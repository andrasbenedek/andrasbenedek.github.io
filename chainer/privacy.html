Chainer privacy policy

Last updated 6 October 2026

In short:

  • The game has no accounts and works offline. Your progress is saved on your phone.
  • Crash reports and gameplay statistics are sent only if you tap SHARE when the game asks. You can turn this off at any time in settings.
  • The free version shows ads from Google AdMob. In the EU, the UK and Switzerland, Google asks for your consent first. Owners of the ad-free pack get no ads, and the ad software never starts for them.
  • No data is sold.

Who is responsible

Chainer is made by András Benedek, an individual developer in Hungary, who is the controller of the personal data described here. Contact: the.chainer.game@gmail.com.

What stays on your phone

Your progress, your settings and whether you own the ad-free pack are stored in the game's own storage on your phone. They are not sent anywhere except in the cases described below: cloud save, if you sign in, and a feedback report, if you send one. Uninstalling the game or clearing its data in Android settings deletes them.

Crash reports and gameplay statistics

On first launch the game asks whether you want to share diagnostics. Nothing is sent before you tap SHARE. If you tap DON'T SHARE, or turn share diagnostics off later in settings, both services below stop.

Legal basis: your consent (Article 6(1)(a) GDPR). You can withdraw it at any time with the settings toggle. Withdrawing does not affect what was sent before.

Sentry: crash reports and feedback

Sentry (Functional Software, Inc.) receives:

  • Crash reports. Each one holds the error and where in the code it happened, your phone model, the Android version, the game's version, and the last few game events before the crash (for example, that a run started).
  • Feedback you send with the send feedback button. A report holds:
    • the text you write, and a name or email address only if you type one in;
    • the game's version and your platform;
    • the map, wave and lives if you send it during a run;
    • your save files (progress, settings, the ad-free pack status and the cloud-save sync record);
    • the game's last 20 errors.

The game does not send your name, email or other identifying details to Sentry unless you type them into feedback. Data is stored in the EU (Germany) and kept for 30 days.

If diagnostics are off, the button reads copy a feedback report and copies the report to your clipboard instead. You decide where to paste it, for example into an email to the address above.

PostHog: gameplay statistics

PostHog (PostHog, Inc.) receives:

  • Gameplay events. For example: a run started, finished or was abandoned, and its map, waves, lives and duration; a chain built; a talent, boost or chain finish bought with in-game currency; a tutorial step reached or skipped; an ad shown; the ad-free pack viewed or bought.
  • App opened and app moved to the background.
  • Device and installation details. A random identifier the analytics software creates for this installation, your phone model, the Android version, the game's version and your language. PostHog also uses your IP address to estimate your country and city.

The identifier is not linked to your name, your Google account or your advertising ID. Data is stored in the EU and kept for up to one year. I use it to find out which parts of the game are too hard, which are confusing, and what to fix next.

Advertising

The free version uses Google AdMob, run by Google Ireland Limited in the EEA and the UK and by Google LLC elsewhere. It shows rewarded ads, which you choose to watch for an in-game reward, and an ad at the end of some runs.

Consent: in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, Google's consent form appears before any ad is requested. You can accept personalised ads, refuse them, or choose purposes one by one. To change your answer later, tap personalised ads · CHANGE in settings. If you refuse personalised ads, Google may still show non-personalised ads, which are chosen by context, such as the app and your rough location. Outside those regions, ads may be personalised as local law permits.

What Google receives: your device's advertising ID, your IP address, device and app information, and how you interact with ads. Google uses it to show ads, measure them, limit how often you see the same one, and prevent fraud. Its use is governed by Google's privacy policy. See how Google uses information from apps that use its services.

You can reset or delete your advertising ID in Android settings, under Privacy → Ads or Google → Ads, depending on your phone.

Legal basis: your consent (Article 6(1)(a) GDPR), collected through Google's form.

Owners of the ad-free pack see no ads, and the game never starts the ad software for them.

Buying the ad-free pack

Payment is handled by Google Play, and I never see your card or payment details.

To check the purchase and let you restore it on another phone, the game uses RevenueCat (RevenueCat, Inc., USA). RevenueCat receives the purchase receipt from Google Play, the product and purchase time, a random identifier it creates for this installation, device and app information, and your IP address. It keeps this for as long as the purchase needs to keep working, and deletes it on request.

Legal basis: performing the contract for the pack (Article 6(1)(b) GDPR).

Cloud save

If you tap sign in under CLOUD SAVE in settings, your progress is copied to Saved Games in your Google Play Games account, so it can move to another phone. It is stored by Google under its privacy policy. You can delete it from the Play Games app's settings. Nothing is uploaded unless you sign in.

Legal basis: providing the feature you asked for (Article 6(1)(b) GDPR).

Google Play

Downloading and updating the game through Google Play is covered by Google's own privacy policy. Google Play gives me statistics, such as install counts, and crash reports that do not identify you.

Transfers outside the EU

Sentry and PostHog store the game's data in the EU. RevenueCat and Google may process data in the United States. Where data leaves the European Economic Area, it is protected by the EU–US Data Privacy Framework or by the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses, whichever the provider uses.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you can:

  • ask for a copy of your data;
  • have your data corrected or deleted;
  • restrict how it is used, or object to that use;
  • receive your data in a portable form;
  • withdraw your consent at any time.

Write to the.chainer.game@gmail.com. The game has no accounts, so tell me roughly when you played or sent feedback, and I will find and delete what I can. I will reply within one month.

You can also complain to a data protection authority: in Hungary, the Nemzeti Adatvédelmi és Információszabadság Hatóság (NAIH), or the authority where you live.

Children

Chainer is not directed at children under 13, and I do not knowingly collect their data. Some countries set a higher age for consenting to data use online: 16 in Hungary and several other EU countries. If you are under that age, tap DON'T SHARE and refuse personalised ads unless a parent agrees. If you believe a child has sent me personal data, for example in feedback, write to me and I will delete it.

Security

Everything the game sends travels encrypted over HTTPS. I do not sell personal data, and I do not use it for automated decisions that affect you.

This website

This site is hosted on GitHub Pages. It uses no cookies, no analytics and no third-party scripts. GitHub may log visitors' IP addresses for security, under the GitHub privacy statement.

Changes

If this policy changes, I will update the date at the top. Where a change needs new consent, I will ask for it before the change applies.
